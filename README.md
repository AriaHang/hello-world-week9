## Idea 1 — Yixing zisha (chosen)

**Concept.** A calm game where you make a Yixing zisha teapot by hand: pound a clay slab into shape, smooth it, and open the kiln to see your pot.

**Why me.** I'm from Yixing, China's "pottery capital." Almost every family there makes teapots. In high school, our military training was making zisha teapots. People even joke: "If you don't study well, you'll go make teapots." Outside Yixing, zisha is seen as an expensive collector's item. I want people to feel its everyday, hand-made side.

**What's different.** Most pottery games use a **spinning wheel** (for example *Let's Create! Pottery*, *Pottery Lab* and *Pot Master 3D*). Zisha is **pounded** from a flat slab around a turntable (打身筒). I couldn't find a game about this.

**Audience and testers.** People who grew up with zisha, people curious about crafts, and cozy-game players. Testers: classmates, and my family in Yixing to check if it feels true.

**Core experience.** Pound the body with your own force and rhythm → smooth it (like the 明针 tool) → open the kiln → keep your pot.

| Must | Should | Nice |
|---|---|---|
| Pounding with force control and a steady rhythm | Smoothing gesture | Pour tea from your pot |
| Sound + visual feedback | Kiln color reveal | A shelf of past pots |
| Crack and dry states, and a way to recover | First-person view, cute style | A friendly voice ("Too hard!") |

**Riskiest unknown.** Can pounding on a screen feel calm and fun? I already ran a first spike (see Proposal details).

**Decision.** **Chosen.** It's personal, rare in games, and small enough to do well.

## Idea 2 — Procrastination × a journey across the earth

**Concept.** A small game for the moment before you start. You type one task, like "write my essay." It gives you **only the first step you can do in two minutes**, like "open the document and type the title." When you finish, your traveler **walks forward on a hand-drawn earth**, and small steps add up to a trip to new places, like a rainy old town or a snowy mountain. You can travel **with friends online**: everyone's steps move the group forward.

**Why me.** I procrastinate, and I love traveling. My teacher liked making this a game. My own doubt: people who can't start don't have the energy to break a task into steps, so here **the tool does the breaking**.

**What's different.** Most to-do apps ask you to plan first. This one starts with one tiny step, and the reward is a journey you can share. I'd read about task initiation and implementation intentions (Gollwitzer).

**Audience and testers.** Students who find it hard to start, including me, and friends who want to keep each other going. Classmates during finals week.

**Core experience.** Type a task → see one 2-minute step → start → finish → your traveler moves forward → arrive in a new place, alone or with friends.

| Must | Should | Nice |
|---|---|---|
| One task → first 2-minute step → timer → move forward | Travel with friends online, with shared progress | A postcard when you arrive |
| Three hand-drawn places | See friends' travelers on the map | Sounds for each place |
| A gentle stop, no punishment | Suggestions made on my laptop (local model), so tasks stay private | Reminders at a chosen time |

**Riskiest unknown.** Does moving forward on a journey really help me start? Online play is also a lot of work. Spike: try three real tasks, with and without the journey, and note how long it takes to begin.

**Decision.** **Backup.** Useful every day and fun to share, but online play and art for every place would take a lot of time. I'd start with one player and three places.

## Idea 3 — A calm world to explore with a friend

**Concept.** Inspired by **NAIAD**, a calm, colorful game by a solo developer where you swim down a river and discover small secrets. NAIAD is played alone. I want to explore this kind of world **with a friend**: one or two hand-made scenes, like a misty bamboo stream or a moonlit lake, where some things only happen **together**, such as singing at the same time to make flowers bloom.

**Why me.** I love traveling and beautiful places, and I want to share calm moments with friends. This idea grew from an earlier "travel the world with family" idea, which was too big. It also uses my strength in visual design.

**What's different.** Most calm exploration games are single-player. Here the core is **one action two people can only do together**.

**Audience and testers.** Friends who like to play together and cozy-game players. At the final show, visitors can play in pairs.

**Core experience.** Two players join → explore a scene → find a spot that needs both → do it together → the scene changes (flowers bloom, light appears) → move on.

| Must | Should | Nice |
|---|---|---|
| One scene, two players on one screen | A second scene | Two phones as controllers |
| One "together" action with clear sound and visuals | Music that changes when players are close | Online play |
| Start, pause and restart | Simple, cute characters | A photo mode |

**Riskiest unknown.** Does doing one thing together feel special, more than playing alone? Spike: flowers bloom only if two people press at the same time; compare with one person alone and ask which felt better.

**Decision.** **Second backup.** It would look great and be fun to show, but games like this can be **pretty but empty**. Reviews of NAIAD praised its art and sound but said the gameplay was weaker, so the shared action would need to be strong.

## How I chose

| | Experience | Scope | Riskiest unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Zisha** | Making by hand, calm and repeatable | Small: one core action | Can pounding feel calm and fun? |
| **2. Journey** | A tiny start every day, shared with friends | Medium | Does moving forward help people start? |
| **3. Calm world** | Beautiful scenes, shared with a friend | Medium | Is it more than pretty? |

I wanted something meaningful, something people **play more than once**, something only I can make, and something I can finish well by December 14. Zisha meets all four. Cozy "making" games I enjoy, like step-by-step cake games, showed me that a calm, repeatable making loop can be fun.

**Ideas I ruled out:** an online "Dream Town" (too big, and similar co-op games like Stardew Valley exist); a planet-hopping game (pretty, but I wasn't sure people would come back; its calm style shapes Idea 1's art); a focus soundscape (too plain alone, so it may become Idea 1's sound layer); a generative typography tool (too close to a classmate's project); a poster-to-design-system tool (not fun enough for me); a spending tracker (a general AI chat already does most of it); and an interactive story about procrastination (people usually play a story only once).

## Proposal details for Idea 1

### Scope

**One-sentence core:** *Pound a zisha teapot body into shape with your own rhythm and force, and feel the clay respond.*

**Stopping condition.** One full round works and feels calm: pound → smooth → open the kiln. When 2–3 testers finish a pot and say they **want to make another one**, the core is done.

**What I would cut first.** Pouring tea and the shelf, then the first-person view. I would never cut the pounding feel.

### System plan

- **Components:** the clay body (its shape changes with every hit), input (force, height, rhythm), clay state (moisture, strain, cracks), and feedback (sound made in the browser, paddle, dents and clay bits).
- **Data:** only the current round, and later a list of finished pots saved on the device. No accounts.
- **Tools:** HTML, CSS and JavaScript (Canvas 2D), maybe light 3D later; GitHub for versions; Figma for sketches.
- **Sources:** my own and my family's experience; a NetEase report on a zisha study trip in Yixing (the body is pounded; 打身筒 should be done in one go; a ceramics teacher needed five or six bodies to learn it); and the wheel-based games named above. All sounds are made in code.
- **Resources:** my laptop and iPhone, video calls with family in Yixing, and maybe an Android phone for a vibration test.

### Spike: can pounding a zisha body feel right on a screen?

**Question.** Which feedback makes pounding feel right: sound, visuals, or vibration?

**Prediction (written before trying).** *Sound can imitate part of the feeling, but it needs visuals too. I hope vibration can be added.*

**Setup.** A small web page: one clay body on a turntable and a target shape. Click high to shape the shoulder, low for the base; too fast cracks the clay, too slow dries it. Three conditions: **A** sound only, **B** sound + visuals, **C** + vibration. I tried A and B on my laptop, and C on my laptop and iPhone. (The code is not part of this submission; I can show it in class.)

| Prediction | What happened |
|---|---|
| Sound can imitate part of it | Partly. Sound alone was not enough. |
| It needs visuals | Yes. **Sound + visuals is needed.** |
| I hope to add vibration | **No vibration** on my laptop or iPhone. My phone's browser can't vibrate from a web page. |
| (not predicted) | **Tapping again and again made my finger tired.** It felt like work, not calm. |
| (not predicted) | **Every hit was the same**, so it got boring. I want to control **how hard** I hit. |

**Decision.** Keep the project and sound + visuals, but change the core action: **pull back and release** to set the force, add a **smooth smoothing gesture**, and use a **first-person view**. Vibration stays a "nice" for a native app.

**Established:** sound + visuals are needed; vibration isn't available in my phone's browser; one tap per hit feels tiring and flat. **Not established:** whether force control and smoothing make it calm and fun. For the Nov 30 prototype, I'll ask 2–3 testers: **"Do you want to make another one?"**
